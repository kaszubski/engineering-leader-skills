# Situational Leadership II (SLII)

> **Source:** SLII® — developed by Ken Blanchard and the founding associates of The Ken Blanchard Companies. See *Leadership and the One Minute Manager*. [blanchard.com](https://www.blanchard.com/)
> A distillation for application. SLII® is a registered model; read the source for the full treatment.

## The operative core

There is no single best leadership style. The right style depends on the person's **development level on a specific task** — not on the person in general.

### Development levels (the person, on one task)

Each level combines **competence** (skill at the task) and **commitment** (motivation + confidence):

| Level | Competence | Commitment | Looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| **D1** | Low | High | Enthusiastic beginner — eager, hasn't done it yet |
| **D2** | Low–some | Low | Disillusioned learner — hit the hard part, confidence dropped |
| **D3** | Moderate–high | Variable | Capable but cautious — can do it, doubts it |
| **D4** | High | High | Self-reliant achiever — competent and confident |

### Leadership styles (what you provide)

| Style | For | You provide |
|---|---|---|
| **S1 Directing** | D1 | Specific instruction, close follow-up |
| **S2 Coaching** | D2 | Direction *and* support — explain why, rebuild confidence |
| **S3 Supporting** | D3 | Encouragement, shared decisions — step back from directing |
| **S4 Delegating** | D4 | Hand over the outcome — get out of the way |

Match the style to the level: D1→S1, D2→S2, D3→S3, D4→S4.

## How skills in this collection use it

1. **Diagnose per task, not per person.** The same engineer can be D4 on React and D1 on incident command. Always anchor to a specific task or skill area.
2. **Recommend the matching style** with *concrete behaviours*, not just the label.
3. **Flag the classic errors** — managing everyone the same way; delegating to a D1 (sink-or-swim); over-directing a D4 (micromanagement, demotivation).
