# Radical Candor

> **Source:** *Radical Candor* — Kim Scott (2017). [radicalcandor.com](https://www.radicalcandor.com/)
> A distillation for application. Read the book for the full treatment.

## The operative core

Good feedback sits on two independent axes:

- **Care Personally** — you give a damn about the person, not just the work.
- **Challenge Directly** — you say the hard thing clearly, without hedging.

Plot them and you get a 2×2:

|  | **Challenge Directly** | **Don't challenge** |
|---|---|---|
| **Care Personally** | **Radical Candor** | **Ruinous Empathy** |
| **Don't care** | **Obnoxious Aggression** | **Manipulative Insincerity** |

- **Radical Candor** — care *and* challenge. The target.
- **Ruinous Empathy** — you care, so you soften the real problem until it disappears. The most common failure for kind people. A genuine defect framed as "nit:" is ruinous empathy.
- **Obnoxious Aggression** — you challenge without care: a pile of criticism, no priority, no respect. "Front-stabbing."
- **Manipulative Insincerity** — neither. Vague praise, political silence.

## How skills in this collection use it

The framework must **change the output**, not decorate it:

1. **Challenge Directly** → every critical point names a *specific* impact and a concrete change. No "consider maybe", no vague unease.
2. **Care Personally** → framing assumes competence and a shared goal. Comment on the work, not the person.
3. **Name the failure mode** → the skill self-checks: is any comment drifting into Ruinous Empathy (a real problem softened to a nit) or Obnoxious Aggression (unprioritised nitpicking)? Flag it.
4. **Separate blocking from non-blocking** → candor without priority is just noise.
